## Salary & Compensation Structure

Understanding the difference between what a company spends and what arrives in a bank account is critical for budgeting.

| Term                      | Definition                                                                           | Key Metric / Impact                                                                                    |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **CTC (Cost to Company)** | The total annual micro-economic cost an employer incurs to employ an individual.     | Includes gross salary, direct benefits, and statutory contributions.                                   |
| **Gross Salary**          | Total salary earned before any tax deductions or voluntary retirement contributions. | Excludes employer-paid benefits like gratuity or health insurance premiums.                            |
| **Net / In-Hand Salary**  | The actual cash amount credited to the bank account each month.                      | Calculated as: $\text{Gross Salary} - \text{TDS} - \text{EPF Contribution} - \text{Professional Tax}$. |

---

## Supplementary Compensation

* **ESOP (Employee Stock Ownership Plan):** Rights given to employees to purchase a specific number of company shares at
  a pre-determined price (exercise price) after a specific timeframe.
* **Vesting Period:** The mandatory duration an employee must remain with the company before they gain ownership of
  their allocated ESOPs.
* **Cliff:** The minimum period (usually one year) an employee must complete before any portion of their ESOPs vest.
* **Perquisites (Perks):** Non-cash benefits provided by an employer (e.g., company-provided accommodation, fuel
  reimbursement, or gadgets) that carry specific tax liabilities.

---

## Taxation & Payroll Deductions

* **TDS (Tax Deducted at Source):** The monthly income tax deducted directly by the employer based on the individual's
  projected annual income slab and deposited with the tax authority.
* **Form 16:** A mandatory certificate issued by the employer at the end of the financial year. It details the total
  salary paid and the exact amount of TDS deducted, serving as the foundational document for filing an Income Tax Return
  (ITR).
* **Financial Year (FY) vs. Assessment Year (AY):** The FY is the period in which income is actively earned (e.g., April
  1 to March 31). The AY is the subsequent year in which that income is evaluated and taxed.
* **Tax Exemptions:** Specific components of a salary structure (such as House Rent Allowance or Leave Travel Allowance)
  that are completely excluded from taxable income, subject to specific compliance rules.
* **Tax Deductions:** Allowable reductions to total taxable gross income achieved by utilizing specific investment
  instruments or incurring valid expenses (e.g., tuition fees, home loan principal).

---

## Statutory & Retirement Benefits

* **EPF (Employees' Provident Fund):** A mandatory savings scheme where both the employee and the employer contribute an
  equal percentage (typically 12% of the basic salary) toward a retirement corpus.
* **Gratuity:** A statutory benefit paid by an employer as a lump sum to an employee when they leave the organization,
  provided they have completed a minimum of 5 years of continuous service.
* **NPS (National Pension System):** A voluntary, defined-contribution retirement savings scheme regulated by the
  government, allowing exposure to equity and debt markets with an additional tax deduction lock-in.

---

## Core Investment & Banking Products

* **Emergency Fund:** A liquid cash reserve equivalent to 3 to 6 months of mandatory living expenses, strictly reserved
  for unplanned events like job loss or medical crises.
* **Liquid Funds:** A category of debt mutual funds investing in short-term market instruments (maturities up to 91
  days). They function as an alternative to savings accounts, offering high liquidity with slightly optimal yields.
* **Mutual Funds:** An investment vehicle that pools capital from multiple investors to purchase a diversified portfolio
  of stocks, bonds, or short-term debt.
* **SIP (Systematic Investment Plan):** An automated investment method where a fixed financial amount is directed into a
  selected mutual fund at regular intervals (usually monthly), utilizing dollar-cost/rupee-cost averaging.
* **PPF (Public Provident Fund):** A long-term, government-backed sovereign savings instrument with a fixed lock-in
  period (typically 15 years), offering tax-free interest and maturity amounts.

---

## Insurance & Risk Management

* **Term Insurance:** A pure life insurance product providing a fixed financial payout to nominees only if the insured
  individual passes away during the policy term. It accumulates no cash or maturity value, making it highly
  cost-effective.
* **Health Insurance (Mediclaim):** An insurance policy covering hospitalization and medical expenses. Relying solely on
  a corporate group health policy is a risk, as coverage ceases immediately upon resignation or termination. Personal
  health cover acts as the primary safety net.
