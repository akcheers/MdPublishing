## NPS – National Pension Scheme

A low-cost, government-backed retirement and investment product regulated by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). Available to all Indian citizens aged 18 to 70.

### Key Features

* **Tax Benefits:**
* **Self Contributions:**
* **Old Tax Regime:** Available. Claim up to ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80CCD(1) (within the overall ₹1.5 lakh Section 80C limit) plus an additional ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B).
* **New Tax Regime:** Not available for self-contributions.


* **Employer Contribution:**
* **Old Tax Regime:** Available.
* **New Tax Regime:** Available (the "Golden Exception"). Employer contributions to your NPS account are tax-deductible under Section 80CCD(2).


* **Tax Status:** Categorized as Exempt, Exempt, Exempt (EEE)—contributions, returns, and final maturity withdrawals (up to limits) are tax-exempt.


* **Returns:** Features higher equity exposure than PPF, historically delivering 9–12% annual returns over the past decade (market-linked, non-guaranteed).
* **Risk Assessment:** Equity exposure is capped between 50% and 75%. After age 50, the equity cap decreases by 2.5% annually, stabilizing at 50% for investors aged 60 and above to reduce risk.

## Types of NPS Accounts

| Feature | Tier I Account | Tier II Account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Account Type** | Mandatory core retirement account. | Voluntary companion investment account. |
| **Lock-in Period** | Locked until age 60 (conditional partial withdrawals permitted). | No lock-in period; withdraw anytime. |
| **Tax Exemption** | Eligible for tax deductions under Section 80CCD. | No tax exemptions on contributions. |
| **Equity Rules** | Equity cap auto-tapers after age 50. | Equity cap auto-tapers after age 50. |

> **NPS Tier II vs. Mutual Funds:** Tier II functions like a low-cost mutual fund. While its expense ratios are among the lowest globally, withdrawals are added to your taxable income and taxed per your applicable slab rate—making it less tax-efficient than equity mutual funds (taxed at 12.5% LTCG).

## Investment Options

Both Tier I and Tier II accounts invest across four distinct asset classes, managed as separate portfolios with no annual ceiling on Tier I contributions:

* **Asset Class E (Equity):** High risk 🟠 (Stocks; capped at 75% for Tier I).
* **Asset Class C (Corporate Debt):** Moderate risk 🟡 (Public and private sector fixed-income instruments).
* **Asset Class G (Government Securities):** Lowest risk 🟢 (Central and State Government bonds with sovereign guarantees).
* **Asset Class A (Alternative Investment Funds):** Highest risk 🔴 (Real estate, infrastructure, and private equity; capped at 5%).

### Choice Strategies

* **Active Choice:** Manually allocate percentages across E, C, G, and A (Equity capped at 75%).
* **Auto Choice:** Automated age-based rebalancing across three risk profiles (Aggressive, Moderate, Conservative) that progressively shifts capital from Equity to Debt as you age.

## Maturity, Exit & Partial Withdrawals

Upon reaching age 60, subscribers face specific exit regulations:

* **Lump Sum:** Up to 60% of the total corpus can be withdrawn completely tax-free.
* **Annuity Purchase:** A minimum of 40% of the corpus must be used to purchase an annuity from an Annuity Service Provider (ASP) to fund a recurring monthly pension.

### Premature Exit Rules (Before Age 60)

* **Minimum Tenure:** Must complete at least 5 years of NPS membership.
* **The 80% Rule:** A minimum of 80% of the corpus must be used to purchase an annuity; only 20% can be taken as a lump sum.
* **Account Closure:** Premature exit permanently closes the associated Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN).

## Annuities Explained

An annuity is a financial contract where a lump sum is paid to an insurance company in exchange for a guaranteed life-long income stream.

### Monthly Pension Determinants

1. **Corpus Size:** Total capital deployed toward the annuity.
2. **Annuity Rate:** Interest rate locked in at purchase for life.
3. **Annuity Option:** The specific structure of the payout plan.

### Core Annuity Structures

* **Life Annuity:** Yields the highest monthly payout; payments stop upon the subscriber's death with no return of principal.
* **Life Annuity with Return of Purchase Price (ROP):** Pays a lifelong pension; upon death, the original purchase principal is refunded to the nominee.
* **Joint Life Annuity:** Pension continues to the surviving spouse at a full or reduced rate (e.g., 50% or 100%) after the subscriber passes away.
* **Joint Life with ROP:** Covers both subscriber and spouse; the full principal is refunded to the nominee after both pass away.

### Payout Details & Taxation

* **Frequencies:** Monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, or annually.
* **Taxation:** Pension payouts are treated as income from salary/other sources and taxed at your applicable tax slab rate.

## Strategic Annuity Execution

* **Age vs. Payout Yield (The Payout Gap):** Insurers calculate payouts based on life expectancy. Purchasing early results in lower annual yields (e.g., ~4–5% at age 45 vs. ~6–7% at age 60 and ~8–9% at age 70).
* **Locking Interest Rates:** Annuity contracts lock in prevailing interest rates permanently. Deploying capital during high-interest-rate market cycles maximizes lifetime pension yields.
* **Annuity Laddering:** For corpuses exceeding ₹10 lakh, PFRDA rules allow splitting funds across multiple annuity providers or plans (minimum ₹5 lakh per option). This can be paired with private annuity purchases across different years to average out long-term interest rate risk.
