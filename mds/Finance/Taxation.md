## 1. Pension Schemes: EPF vs. Corporate NPS

While EPF is a mandatory salary component, Corporate NPS serves as an optional, highly tax-efficient retirement vehicle.

### The Core Difference

* **Employee Provident Fund (EPF):** Mandatory for most salaried employees (12% of Basic Salary). Non-negotiable and built into your base CTC structure.
* **Corporate NPS (National Pension System):** Optional flexi-benefit component allowed by employers.
* **Private Sector:** Employers can contribute up to **10% of Basic Salary**.
* **Government Sector:** Employers can contribute up to **14% of Basic Salary**.
* **Tax Advantage:** Employer contribution to Corporate NPS is eligible for tax deduction under **Section 80CCD(2)** over and above standard limits.

## 2. Mutual Fund & ETF Taxation

In India, mutual funds are taxed strictly based on **asset class composition and holding periods**, rather than whether they are actively managed or passively tracked (Index Funds).

### Equity vs. Debt Mutual Funds

| Feature / Criteria | Equity-Oriented Funds | Debt-Oriented Funds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Asset Criteria** | Invests $\ge$ 65% in Indian equities | Invests < 35% in equities |
| **Short-Term Holding Period** | $\le$ 12 months | Any holding period |
| **STCG Tax Rate** | **20%** | Income Tax Slab Rate |
| **Long-Term Holding Period** | > 12 months | Not Applicable *(Units bought post-April 1, 2023)* |
| **LTCG Tax Rate** | **12.5%** *(Gains above ₹1.25 Lakh/year)* | Income Tax Slab Rate |

> **Mutual Funds vs. Index Funds:** Index funds are simply passive mutual funds. An equity index fund and an active equity fund follow the exact same tax rules.

---

### Gold ETFs & Gold Mutual Funds

Gold-backed paper instruments are taxed under refined listed-security rules:

* **Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG):** Held for **$\le$ 12 months** $\rightarrow$ Taxed at your applicable **Income Tax Slab Rate**.
* **Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG):** Held for **> 12 months** $\rightarrow$ Taxed at **12.5%** (without indexation).

## 3. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS)

TDS is an advance tax collection mechanism where the payer (**deductor**) subtracts tax before disbursing the payout to the recipient (**deductee**).

### Core Definitions & Framework

* **Deductor:** Entity responsible for making the payment and depositing tax (e.g., employer, bank, business).
* **Deductee:** Individual or entity receiving the income (e.g., employee, contractor).
* **TDS Certificate:** Proof of tax deduction issued by the deductor (e.g., **Form 16** for salary, **Form 16A** for non-salary income).
* **Form 26AS / AIS:** Official tax portal statements reflecting all TDS credited against your PAN.
* **Governing Framework:** As of April 1, 2026, direct taxes and TDS operations are governed by the **Income Tax Act, 2025** (replacing the historical 1961 Act).

---

### TDS on Fixed Deposits (FDs)

Banks and financial institutions deduct tax on FD interest earnings as it accrues:

* **Standard Rate (with PAN):** **10% TDS** on annual interest accrued.
* **Higher Rate (without PAN):** **20% TDS**.
* **Cumulative FDs:** Tax is deducted **annually** on accrued interest, even if the payout occurs at the end of a multi-year term.

---

## 4. OLTAS (Online Tax Accounting System)

OLTAS is the digital infrastructure used by the Income Tax Department to track, collect, and acknowledge direct tax payments online.

* **Challan Identification Number (CIN):** A unique identifier generated upon every successful tax payment that acts as legal proof of deposit.
* **Taxpayer Verification:** Payment receipts and CIN status can be independently tracked and verified via the official tax portal (TIN-NSDL / e-Filing portal).
